Texas public works

User Manual

Prepare a takeoff, review the public bids behind its prices, and build your opinion of probable construction cost (OPCC).

Keep your estimate open in its original tab while you read this guide.

1. Quick start

  1. Read a takeoff. Paste rows from Excel, upload a schedule, or use the takeoff template. Each pay item needs a description, unit and quantity.
  2. Review the items. Check quantities, units and item types. Use Change for a wrong classification and resolve any line marked check.
  3. Set project details. Choose the market and bid date, review the expected bidders, and enter owner-set amounts and vendor quotes where needed.
  4. Press Estimate. Review the model comparison totals and any warnings. The worksheet loads recent matching public bid medians separately.
  5. Finish your OPCC. Review the three selected reference projects, enter prices that need your judgment, choose Construction Contingency, and download the Excel workbook or PDF.

Reading a takeoff classifies its lines. It does not price them until you press Estimate.

2. Takeoff inputs

The Excel template provides the basic columns. Fill in one pay item per row, then paste the rows with their headers or upload the file. Pasted text may use tabs, commas, pipes or aligned columns.

Item
The pay-item number. Use Renumber items if the displayed order should become the numbering order.
Description
The work or product, including material and size when relevant. More specific wording helps match the right earlier bids.
Unit and quantity
The measurement and amount of work, such as CY, SY, LF, EA or LS. Check what was read, especially from scanned documents.
Amount
Read only for an owner-set allowance, contingency or force account. Other uploaded prices do not become the model's estimate. Quantity is never treated as dollars.

Column order does not matter. Excel, CSV, PDF and Word schedules can be uploaded. Scanned PDF and image reading uses the local OCR tools when available; inspect the extracted lines before estimating.

Rows that are not treated as pay items are available under Rows not treated as pay items. Add missing pay items with Add row. A line without a quantity is left out until it is corrected.

3. Review the classification

The item type, unit and size determine which earlier bids are comparable. Check the Classification column before estimating. A correct-looking description can still have the wrong item type.

If none of the suggested items fits, choose New item / Other — Engineer input beside the suggestions or in Change. After estimating, this line's Your $/unit starts blank for your judgment. Any model price is a reference only; it is not automatically copied into your OPCC. Enter a price before treating the worksheet total as complete.

Not covered
There is almost no comparable bid history for this kind of item. Get a vendor quote; the model totals exclude it.
Low confidence
A lump sum that bidders price very differently. Review the historical range and use your judgment.
Owner-set
Money set by the owner, such as an allowance, contingency or force account. Enter the stated amount.
Unclassified
No suitable item type was found. Use Change to choose one.
Engineer input
You selected New item / Other because no item type matched. Enter your own worksheet unit price; choosing a normal classification removes this selection.
Check
The reader is unsure about something on this line. Review the stated reason against the schedule.
Remembered
An earlier classification choice for the same wording has been applied. Confirm that it fits this item too.
Read by Claude
Read from a PDF by Claude. Check the items and quantities against the document.

Warnings name the affected items and the action to take. Acknowledging a warning records your review; it does not repair an input or add a missing price.

4. Project details

Market
City / County is the default. Choose TxDOT for that market. Not sure uses City / County.
Owner
Optional. Earlier jobs of the same owner receive more weight when similar projects are selected. Pick an owner or type one that is not listed.
Work type
Optional. Earlier jobs of the same work type receive more weight in similar-project selection. An unlisted work type uses the generic match.
Bid date
Prices are adjusted to its year. Comparable bids must have been made before this date. The page starts from your local calendar date.
Expected bidders
The suggested count comes from earlier jobs after the takeoff is read. Edit it when you have better information. The bidder count affects the typical gap between the median bidder and the low bid.
Owner-set amounts
Enter the actual allowance or other stated amount. When asked, press Re-estimate to include an edited amount in the model totals.
Vendor quotes
Enter quotes for not-covered lines. Each line has one shared amount, wherever you edit it. Quotes are added separately to the worksheet and complete comparison totals.

The estimate targets the lowest of the expected bids. More bidders generally produce a lower expected low bid relative to the median bidder. The competition adjustment applies to the bid total; owner-set money is added afterward.

There is no input for your earlier OPCC or an engineer's estimate. Engineer estimates are a yardstick for evaluation, never an input to pricing.

5. P50 and P70

P50: expected low bid

The middle estimate of the lowest bid. In the idealized picture, the low bid is equally likely to fall above or below it. Use it as a central estimate.

P70: trial recommended OPCC

A higher figure with a nominal 70% target: the low bid at or below the estimate on 7 out of 10 jobs. This local trial has not been independently validated at that hit rate.

P70 does not mean “P50 plus 70%.” Its margin comes from the 30th percentile of earlier public forward-scored ratios of P50 to the actual low bid. It is calibrated separately by market; it does not currently widen for an unusual individual job or narrow for a well-referenced one.

In the left picture, the entire area represents possible low bids: 50% lies below P50, another 20% between P50 and P70, and 30% above P70. The right picture shows the same shares as a running total.

Expected low bid (P50) and Recommended OPCC (P70) are model estimates for the project. The recent-bid card adds up quantity × recent bid median for the items with enough matching bids. Its item count shows how much of the schedule it covers.

The 50% and 80% historical ranges describe two-sided intervals. Each budget percentile gives a single upper threshold.

The 70% target is not an independently verified P70 success rate or a guarantee for one project. P70 can still fall short. Missing scope and not-covered lines require separate attention; add their prices before treating an OPCC as complete.

6. How prices are calculated

  1. Match each covered line to earlier public bid items using its wording, item type, unit and size. Adjust the reference price to the bid year.
  2. Add the line amounts and apply the historical competition discount for the expected bidder count. This produces a line-based estimate of the low bid.
  3. Combine that estimate with the price implied by similar earlier projects, using a geometric mean when comparable pricing is available.
  4. Apply the market's level correction, measured from earlier estimation errors. Add owner-set money at face value to obtain P50.
  5. For the local P70 trial, divide headline P50 by the 30th-percentile ratio of P50 to actual low bid from earlier public forward results.

Line-based bid = sum of covered reference amounts × competition discount

Combined bid = √(line-based bid × similar-project bid)

P50 = combined bid × level correction + owner-set money

Trial P70 = headline P50 ÷ 30th-percentile historical ratio (P50 ÷ actual low bid)

The line-based estimate is used when comparable pricing is unavailable. Some price adjustments are market-specific. The formulas summarize the process; the project's source documents describe the individual rules.

Owner-set money is not competition-discounted or level-corrected. It is added after those steps. The headline P70 is a calibrated total; it is not an instruction to increase every fixed allowance. In your worksheet, owner-set line amounts remain at face value and do not receive the recent-bid or project-level model multiplier.

Not-covered items remain outside model P50 and P70. Vendor quotes are added at face value to the complete totals. The model uses public bids only; your worksheet prices never train or tune it.

7. Your OPCC worksheet

On a phone or tablet, each pay item has its own card. Edit Your $/unit beside Your amount. Tap the description to see the bids behind the price.

New worksheets start from Recent bid median. In Estimate, select P50, P70 or the recent-bid card at any time to change the source of automatic unit prices. All three references stay visible for comparison. Your own prices, individual reference picks, vendor quotes and owner-set amounts stay in the worksheet.

For each eligible item, the tool selects three distinct public projects in the same market with closely matching work, unit, size and quantity. Each must have a precise bid date before your bid date and within the preceding two calendar years. Eligible projects from the most recent year are preferred. Older projects are not added to make up a missing third reference.

Each project's own bidder median is adjusted to your bid year before taking the median of the three adjusted values. The adjustment uses an annual index of matching public bids dated before your bid date, with at least ten matched items for each annual step. The factor can raise or lower a price. Original bidder medians, dates, factors and the index evidence remain attached to the selected prices.

If fewer than three eligible projects are available, or a required price adjustment is unavailable, the default stays blank. Review the available references and enter your unit price. Loading or missing references never cause a model P70 price to be substituted.

Recent bid median
The middle of three matching projects' adjusted median unit prices. Open the line to see each project's bids and price adjustment.
P50 and P70 model unit prices
The P50 unit price comes from the model. The P70 unit price applies the project's P70 multiplier to that P50 price. Both use the pay item's unit. The percentiles describe the project's low bid.
Your $/unit
The price used in your OPCC. You can keep the recent bid median, choose another source from Reference, or enter your own price.
Rounding
The default uses unit-price cents, so quantity times the visible unit price equals the line amount. The full adjusted median is retained in the source record. You may choose rounding up or to a convenient amount; a price you type is never automatically rounded.
Engineer input
Mobilization and temporary erosion-control lump sums remain your input. An explicitly selected New item / Other also starts blank. Your entered prices survive reference loading and re-estimation.
Missing prices
A blank price is excluded from Your OPCC. Complete these lines before exporting a finished estimate.
Your OPCC
Your line amounts, plus the schedule's contingency amounts and any additional Construction Contingency. It is independent of the model P50, P70 and retained P80 totals.

When you re-estimate, automatically selected recent medians refresh from the new inputs. Typed prices, deliberate reference picks, notes, vendor quotes and owner-set amounts are retained. New reference responses cannot replace a price you entered while they were loading.

The existing Reference list also contains explicitly labeled model and other historical choices. Selecting one is your decision; it does not change the project's model totals. Saved P50, P70 or P80 worksheets retain their original basis rather than being relabeled as recent bid medians.

Fill in Project, Prepared by and Project no. for the exported OPCC. Your worksheet edits affect only your OPCC and its exports, never the model's reference tables.

8. Construction Contingency

Construction Contingency covers what the estimate cannot see yet: scope that may change, unknown site conditions and omitted items. It is separate from P70's margin for pricing uncertainty.

A percentage contingency is calculated from your worksheet subtotal and shown below it. The project total is the subtotal plus the contingency amounts. The default additional contingency is 10% when the schedule has no contingency allowance.

If the schedule already carries a contingency allowance, additional contingency starts at 0% to avoid counting it twice. Add more only for risks that allowance does not cover.

P70 plus a contingency can be useful while design scope is changing. For a final design, adding more for the same pricing uncertainty can raise the total without covering a new risk. Review what each allowance is intended to cover.

The historical accuracy tables describe the project model, not the recent-bid worksheet defaults. They show how often several percentages above model P50 still fell below the low bid. They do not measure unknown scope or recommend a contingency for your specific project.

9. Reference evidence

Open a worksheet line to see the Recent bid median. Each project card identifies the original pay item, its quantity and the number of distinct bidders who quoted it. Median at bid date is the middle of those bidders’ unit prices. With an even number of prices, take the average of the two middle values. Multiply this median by the Price adjustment to express it in your bid year's dollars. A factor of 1 leaves the price unchanged.

Each qualifying project has one vote. For example, if three adjusted project medians are $27.03, $33.50 and $42.91 per CY, the recent bid median is $33.50/CY. The recent reference range is $27.03–$42.91/CY. The calculation uses the underlying precision before the result is displayed in cents. A missing third project or missing adjustment leaves the recent reference price unavailable.

Historical bids behind model P50 can include older projects. Each row summarizes the matching unit-price quotes in one historical project. The model can group several similar items from the same bid tab. Open the project name for the complete bid tab or the bid count for individual quotes.

Project low bid (total)
The lowest recorded bid for the entire historical project, in dollars.
Average and median bid $/unit
The arithmetic mean and middle value of the matching unit quotes at the time of bidding. A second value labeled with a year is the equivalent rate in that year's dollars.
Project low bidder’s $/unit
The matching unit rate from the contractor with the lowest project total. When several of that contractor's items match, the table gives their median unit rate.
Lowest–highest $/unit
The smallest and largest matching unit quotes within that one historical project. The bidder offering the smallest unit price can differ from the bidder offering the lowest project total.
Matched quantity
The quantity of the matched historical work in the displayed unit. If the group includes several quantities, the table shows their median.

Review size and unit differences before choosing a price. Converted-unit prices are hints because different units can describe different products.

For horizontal directional drilling (HDD), check which pipe the earlier bidder installed. A utility carrier pipe, a steel casing and a communications duct can all be bid by the linear foot. If the product differs, use a comparable bid or enter an engineer-reviewed price.

Similar past projects shows actual public projects with overlapping work. Their matched lines, full bid tabulations and source links help you explain a choice. A similar project can still differ in scope or site conditions.

Model accuracy is the historical P50 item indicator. It is not a confidence score for the newly selected recent bid median.

Low confidence compares mobilization and general-condition lump sums with the shares carried by earlier low bidders. This is a judgment aid. That panel does not change P50 or P70.

Competition reports the bidder count and historical low-to-median discount used for the estimate. A weak or unavailable discount is flagged rather than silently extrapolated.

10. Historical accuracy

To measure project accuracy, add the estimated amounts for covered pay items other than lump sums. Compare that subtotal with the same items on the original low bidder’s bid tab. The worksheet still includes the full schedule. A covered item marked low confidence stays in the comparison.

City / County development check. Of 235 historical jobs, 174 had enough information to compare the same items. The estimate met or exceeded the comparable bid on 81 jobs at P50 (46.6%), 112 at P70 (64.4%) and 134 at P80 (77.0%). P80’s target is 80%. The check used historical bidder counts and winning unit prices multiplied by quantities.

11. Excel and PDF exports

Download the OPCC (Excel) produces your OPCC with item numbers, descriptions, units, quantities, unit costs and amounts, followed by Sub-Total, Construction Contingency and Project Total. The workbook also includes model comparisons and the public projects behind the prices. Recent-bid sources retain all selected project dates, original bidder medians, adjusted medians and index evidence. P80 remains labeled as the original model baseline.

Download the OPCC (PDF) produces the same OPCC as it prints. Both use US Letter. Review the project details, missing-price warnings and totals before sharing either file.

Workbook formulas also carry saved values so a preview can show the totals without recalculating.

12. Data, privacy and limitations

The model is built from public bid tabulations. Engineer estimates are used only for comparison; company or client bid files and your worksheet prices do not feed the model.

Local mode: takeoff reading and pricing run on your machine. Public demo: uploaded files are processed on the server and are not stored; do not upload confidential documents. Corrections and saved worksheets in the public demo stay in that browser.

Current data dates, OCR availability and software versions are available under About this tool in the estimator.

The tool focuses first on city drainage, creek-erosion and streambank work in the Dallas–Fort Worth area, with TxDOT as another market and fallback price source. Sparse history, specialty equipment, unusual scope and site-dependent lump sums need additional review.

If too much of the schedule is outside the available price history, the tool may withhold P50 and P70. Resolve missing inputs and obtain separate prices rather than treating a partial total as a complete estimate.

The low bid is the lowest recorded bidder total used by the method. It is not necessarily the final award, and it does not measure later construction changes. Review the schedule and evidence before relying on any total.

13. AI mode

AI mode is not available yet. Its intended purpose is to read engineering drawings and documents into a draft takeoff for an estimator to check. The current placeholder and disabled Read by AI button do not provide that feature.

For now, supply a takeoff schedule through Paste text or Upload file and review the items that were read.
